# Worked example: a kids' learning game

The owner dropped two screenshots ("these look sloppy") from a build-and-fly
math game for a 7-year-old and asked for cleaner colorways, organization and
layout, critiqued through Katie Dill, Peter Tarka and Zhenya Rynzhuk.

## Step 0

- Read both screenshots: the mission card and the build screen.
- Searched the screen builders and found eight more surfaces sharing the same
  parts: add-up quiz, "will it fly?", mission complete, try again, paint
  shop, grown-up settings, the title and mission picker, and a button
  inventory.
- Counted in the stylesheet: 14 button looks, 8 corner radii, coral used for
  four meanings, two speaker sizes, disabled at 35% and 55%.
- Audience: one 7-year-old on an iPad, learning to read and add within 20.
  Fixed codes: orange = weight, blue = thrust.

## Crit (excerpt)

**Mission card (seen)**
- *High · Composition.* The mission, "2 unicorns → Lantern Station", is the
  third thing you see, in a white box at the bottom.
- *High · Systems.* Six colors in one card (coral tag, yellow speaker, blue
  hint, white box, beige pill, teal button) and none has a job.
- *Medium · Material.* The mascot balances on the card's corner with its base
  hanging over the edge, like a sticker that slipped.
- *Medium · Composition.* "Your turn to read!" is a blue pill that looks like
  a button but is the mascot talking.

**Will it fly? (from code only)**
- *High · Systems.* "Yes" is green and "No" is coral on a prediction, which
  hints at the right answer.

## Options

Ten surfaces, each with Today plus one or two layouts ("Picture first",
"Mission ticket", "Craft in the middle", "Up and down", "Stars first").
Skins: five colorways (Today, Paper Kite, Night Lantern, Sky Ink, World Tint),
four button styles (Today, Toy Block, Soft Clay, Sticker) and four card styles
(Today, Clean Sheet, Frosted Glass, Sticker). Quick sets: Calm Paper (the
recommendation), World Tint, Lantern Night and Storybook Bold.

## Closing the loop

The owner saved four sets, all Storybook Bold, with two notes:

> I like the mission ticket, I still want my son to be able to read and not
> guess based on the pictures alone… Goal: to help them read.

> Lead with words on the mission card… a button for help where they can read
> it for him with the image and then it flips back to the words.

The "Picture first" proposal conflicted with the goal, so the build changed:
the ticket leads with words, one sentence per line with space between, and
a Help button flips the ticket to the picture side while it reads aloud, then
flips back. The owner also chose Lantern Night for the space levels, so the
design system shipped as two themes on one set of components.
